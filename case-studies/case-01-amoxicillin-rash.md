# Case 01: Amoxicillin-Associated Rash (Fictional)

## Case narrative
A fictional adult patient is prescribed oral amoxicillin for a presumed bacterial respiratory infection. A diffuse maculopapular rash is reported after treatment begins. The available teaching vignette does not provide a precise onset interval, complete examination findings, allergy history, infection work-up, concomitant medicines or a documented dechallenge outcome.

## Evidence review
- Suspected drug: amoxicillin.
- Event: reported maculopapular rash.
- Timing: after treatment began, but exact latency is not supplied.
- Alternative causes: infection-associated rash and other medicines have not been excluded.
- Dechallenge: unknown.
- Rechallenge: not performed; it must not be attempted merely for assessment.

## WHO–UMC assessment
**Provisional category: Possible.** The reported timing is compatible, but alternative explanations remain plausible and withdrawal information is unavailable.

## Information needed
- Exact first-dose and rash-onset dates/times.
- Morphology, distribution, severity and clinician diagnosis.
- Prior beta-lactam exposure and allergy history.
- Infection diagnosis and other potential causes.
- Concomitant medicines and subsequent clinical course.

## Learning point
A plausible drug-event sequence is not enough to establish causality. Document missing information and avoid an unjustified "Certain" classification.
