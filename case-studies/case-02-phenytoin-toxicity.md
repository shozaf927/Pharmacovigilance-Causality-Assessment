# Case 02: Phenytoin-Associated Neurological Toxicity (Fictional)

## Case narrative
A fictional patient taking phenytoin develops nystagmus and ataxia. A serum phenytoin concentration is reported above the laboratory therapeutic range. The vignette does not include the exact concentration, dose history, albumin, renal/hepatic function, interacting medicines or alternative neurological diagnoses.

## Evidence review
- Suspected drug: phenytoin.
- Event: nystagmus and ataxia.
- Objective evidence: elevated serum concentration is reported, but the numeric result and sampling time are absent.
- Plausibility: neurological toxicity is consistent with phenytoin exposure.
- Alternative causes: not fully excluded.
- Dechallenge: not documented.
- Rechallenge: not appropriate solely to test causality.

## WHO–UMC assessment
**Provisional category: Probable/Likely.** The clinical pattern and reported elevated concentration support the relationship. The case is not Certain because key clinical details and exclusion of alternatives are incomplete.

## Information needed
- Actual concentration, units, sampling time and laboratory range.
- Dose, adherence, recent dose changes and formulation.
- Albumin and relevant organ function.
- Interacting drugs and medication reconciliation.
- Neurological assessment and response to dose adjustment/withdrawal.

## Learning point
Therapeutic drug monitoring can strengthen a case, but interpretation depends on sampling time, protein binding and clinical context.
