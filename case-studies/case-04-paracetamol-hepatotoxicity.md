# Case 04: Paracetamol-Associated Hepatotoxicity (Fictional)

## Case narrative
A fictional patient presents with elevated liver enzymes after reported paracetamol use. The amount taken, timing, formulation, duration, possible combination products, alcohol use, baseline liver function and alternative causes are not supplied.

## Evidence review
- Suspected drug: paracetamol (acetaminophen).
- Event: elevated liver enzymes / suspected liver injury.
- Mechanistic plausibility: excessive exposure can increase formation of the reactive metabolite NAPQI and deplete glutathione.
- Exposure: dose and cumulative intake are unknown.
- Alternative causes: viral, autoimmune, ischemic and other drug-related liver injury must be considered.
- Dechallenge: unknown.
- Rechallenge: must not be attempted to establish causality.

## WHO–UMC assessment
**Provisional category: Possible.** Exposure precedes the event and the mechanism is plausible, but dose, latency, diagnostic work-up and alternative causes are not documented.

## Information needed
- Total dose, timing, formulation and all combination products.
- Serum paracetamol concentration interpreted in the correct clinical context.
- ALT, AST, bilirubin, INR and serial trends.
- Baseline liver status and differential diagnosis.
- Clinical treatment, including whether antidotal treatment was required.

## Learning point
A plausible mechanism does not prove that paracetamol caused a specific patient's liver injury. Exposure quantification and differential diagnosis are essential.
