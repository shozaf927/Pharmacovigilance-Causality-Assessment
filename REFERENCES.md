# References and Further Reading

The following primary and authoritative sources underpin this educational project. Consult the latest official versions before applying guidance in practice.

1. **Naranjo CA, Busto U, Sellers EM, et al. (1981).** A method for estimating the probability of adverse drug reactions. *Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics*, 30(2), 239–245. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/clpt.1981.154
2. **WHO–UMC.** The use of the WHO-UMC system for standardised case causality assessment. Uppsala Monitoring Centre: https://who-umc.org/causality-assessment/ (official causality assessment resource).
3. **World Health Organization (2019).** Causality assessment of an adverse event following immunization (AEFI): user manual for the revised WHO classification, 2nd edition. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241516990
4. **International Council for Harmonisation (ICH).** E2A: Clinical Safety Data Management—Definitions and Standards for Expedited Reporting. https://www.ich.org/page/efficacy-guidelines
5. **Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission.** Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI): https://ipc.gov.in/mandates/pvpi.html
6. **U.S. Food and Drug Administration.** Drug safety and adverse event reporting information: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/surveillance
7. **European Medicines Agency.** Good pharmacovigilance practices: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/pharmacovigilance/good-pharmacovigilance-practices

## Citation practice
When using a method, cite its original publication or official guidance. Do not cite secondary summaries as if they were the primary source. Check current versions and local reporting requirements.

## Scope note
This repository is an educational portfolio project. It does not replace institutional SOPs, official reporting forms, regulatory guidance or clinical judgement.
