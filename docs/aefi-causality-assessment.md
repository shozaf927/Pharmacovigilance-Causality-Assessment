# AEFI Causality Assessment

## 1. Definition
An adverse event following immunization (AEFI) is any untoward medical occurrence after immunization; it does not necessarily have a causal relationship with vaccine use. AEFI assessment evaluates whether the event is causally related to the vaccine, immunization process or another cause.

## 2. Why a distinct framework?
Vaccines are administered to large populations, often to healthy people, and events may occur coincidentally after immunization. Assessment therefore needs to consider vaccine product, quality, administration errors, immunization stress-related responses and coincidental disease, in addition to biological plausibility and timing.

## 3. Evidence domains
A structured WHO AEFI review considers:
- Eligibility and quality of the case information.
- Temporal relationship between immunization and event.
- Known association and biological plausibility.
- Evidence from clinical investigation and relevant diagnostic tests.
- Alternative causes and coincidental events.
- Vaccine product, quality and immunization errors.
- Consistency with available epidemiological and prior safety evidence.

The five principles mentioned in the source video—strength, consistency, specificity, temporal relationship and biological plausibility—are useful epidemiological considerations, but should not be treated as the complete WHO AEFI algorithm.

## 4. Common causal explanations to consider
- Vaccine product-related reaction.
- Vaccine quality defect-related reaction.
- Immunization error-related reaction.
- Immunization stress-related response.
- Coincidental event.
- Indeterminate or unclassifiable case, depending on evidence and applicable guidance.

## 5. Assessment workflow
1. Verify immunization details: vaccine, dose, date/time, batch/lot, route, site and administrator where available.
2. Establish event onset, clinical course, diagnosis and outcome.
3. Review medical records, laboratory tests and other relevant investigations.
4. Compare the time interval with known biological and clinical evidence.
5. Assess alternative explanations.
6. Review whether a product or immunization error occurred.
7. Apply the current WHO national AEFI causality assessment guidance and document the rationale.

## 6. Safety and reporting
Do not assume that "after vaccination" means "caused by vaccination." Report and investigate according to national policy. Do not delay urgent clinical care while causality is being assessed.

**Reference:** WHO, *Causality assessment of an adverse event following immunization (AEFI): user manual for the revised WHO classification*, 2nd edition, 2019.
