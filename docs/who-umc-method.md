# WHO–UMC Causality Assessment

## Overview
The WHO–UMC system is a structured clinical judgement framework for classifying the likelihood of a causal relationship between a suspected medicine and an adverse reaction. It considers clinical-pharmacological aspects, timing, dechallenge, alternative explanations and the quality of information.

## Categories
| Category | Operational interpretation |
|---|---|
| Certain | A plausible time relationship; the event cannot be explained by disease or other drugs; response to withdrawal is clinically plausible; the event is definitive pharmacologically or phenomenologically; rechallenge is satisfactory if necessary. |
| Probable / Likely | A reasonable time relationship; disease or other drugs are unlikely explanations; response to withdrawal is clinically reasonable; rechallenge is not required. |
| Possible | A reasonable time relationship; disease or other drugs could also explain the event; withdrawal information may be unclear or absent. |
| Unlikely | A time relationship makes a causal connection improbable; another explanation is more plausible. |
| Conditional / Unclassified | More data are essential for a proper assessment, or additional data are being examined. |
| Unassessable / Unclassifiable | The report suggests an adverse reaction, but information is insufficient or contradictory and cannot be supplemented or verified. |

## Practical assessment sequence
1. Is the event clinically well described?
2. Is the time relationship reasonable for the suspected medicine?
3. Is the reaction compatible with the known pharmacology or safety profile?
4. Are there plausible alternative causes, including underlying disease or concomitant drugs?
5. What happened after withdrawal or dose reduction?
6. Is there naturally occurring or clinically justified rechallenge information?
7. Is the information sufficient to assign a category?

## Distinguishing Certain, Probable and Possible
- **Certain** requires a strong, coherent case with alternative explanations effectively excluded and other defining criteria satisfied.
- **Probable/Likely** is appropriate when timing and clinical course support the relationship and alternatives are unlikely, even without rechallenge.
- **Possible** is appropriate when timing is reasonable but competing causes remain plausible or dechallenge information is unclear.

Do not upgrade a case merely because the reaction is listed in product information. Do not downgrade solely because rechallenge is unavailable.

## Documentation
Record the evidence and reasoning supporting the category, the alternative causes considered, the information still missing and the date of assessment. The category may change when follow-up information becomes available.

## Important
WHO–UMC categories are not direct equivalents of Naranjo score bands. Use the exact terminology of the selected method and report the method used.
