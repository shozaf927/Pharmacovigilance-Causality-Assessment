# Case 04 — Serious, Unexpected Angioedema

## Case narrative
A 51-year-old adult starts fictional medicine **DX-415**. Within 2 hours, the patient develops rapidly progressive tongue and oropharyngeal swelling with respiratory compromise. Emergency airway management and inpatient intensive monitoring are required. The patient recovers and is discharged. No prior similar episode is reported.

## Hypothetical RSI excerpt
“Transient mild cutaneous rash has been reported. Angioedema, airway compromise and anaphylactic reactions are not described.”

This excerpt is fictional. It is intentionally constructed to test the nature/severity comparison.

## Classification
| Dimension | Result | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Severity | Severe | Rapid progression, respiratory compromise and intensive intervention |
| Seriousness | Serious | Life-threatening at the time and inpatient hospitalisation; either criterion is sufficient |
| Expectedness | Unexpected | Angioedema with airway compromise is not consistent with the supplied RSI |
| Causality | Not determined here | Close temporal relationship supports investigation, but full assessment is still required |

## Clinical impact
Critical, potentially fatal clinical impact. Immediate clinical management takes priority over documentation. The case warrants urgent medical and pharmacovigilance review under applicable procedures.

## Follow-up / review
Capture exact onset, airway findings, oxygenation, blood pressure, interventions, concomitant medicines, previous exposure, allergy history, complement/C1-inhibitor investigations if clinically indicated, dechallenge and outcome. Do not deliberately rechallenge a patient after a potentially life-threatening reaction.

## Teaching point
This is the classic **serious + unexpected** pattern, but the classification is based on two independent decisions: seriousness criteria and RSI comparison.
