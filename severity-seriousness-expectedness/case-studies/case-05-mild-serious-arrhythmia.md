# Case 05 — Mild Symptoms, Serious Due to Inpatient Hospitalisation

## Case narrative
A 59-year-old adult taking fictional medicine **EX-520** reports brief palpitations without pain or breathlessness. The patient appears comfortable and describes the symptoms as mild. An ECG obtained during assessment shows a new clinically significant arrhythmia. The treating clinician admits the patient as an inpatient for continuous cardiac monitoring and treatment because of the documented arrhythmia. The patient is discharged after 24 hours.

## Hypothetical RSI excerpt
“Palpitations without documented arrhythmia may occur. Clinically significant arrhythmia requiring inpatient monitoring is not described.”

This excerpt is fictional.

## Classification
| Dimension | Result | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Severity | Mild symptoms; objective abnormality clinically significant | The patient's subjective symptom intensity is mild; objective findings must be separately documented |
| Seriousness | Serious | Inpatient hospitalisation is required for the event |
| Expectedness | Unexpected | The supplied RSI describes palpitations only, not the documented arrhythmia requiring inpatient monitoring |
| Causality | Not determined here | Temporal association is insufficient; evaluate disease, electrolytes, cardiac history and concomitant drugs |

## Clinical impact
Potentially high despite mild subjective symptoms. The objective ECG finding and inpatient management determine clinical significance and seriousness.

## Teaching point
A mild symptom description does not prevent an event from being serious. The event term and objective diagnosis matter; seriousness is not assigned by the patient's adjective alone.
