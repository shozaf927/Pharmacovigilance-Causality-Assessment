# Case 03 — Serious, Expected Gastrointestinal Bleeding

## Case narrative
A 67-year-old adult receiving fictional anticoagulant **CX-330** develops melena and symptomatic anaemia. The patient is admitted as an inpatient for 3 days, receives clinically indicated treatment and is discharged after stabilisation. No fatal outcome or persistent disability is reported.

## Hypothetical RSI excerpt
“Gastrointestinal haemorrhage, including melena and clinically significant bleeding requiring hospitalisation, is an identified adverse reaction.”

This excerpt is fictional and not a statement about any marketed anticoagulant.

## Classification
| Dimension | Result | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Severity | Severe | Clinically significant bleeding with anaemia and inpatient treatment |
| Seriousness | Serious | Requires inpatient hospitalisation |
| Expectedness | Expected | Nature and clinically significant/hospitalised manifestation are explicitly described in the supplied RSI |
| Causality | Not determined here | Anticoagulant exposure is relevant, but alternative causes and full clinical evidence must be assessed |

## Clinical impact
High clinical impact due to bleeding, anaemia and inpatient care. Stabilisation does not reverse the seriousness classification.

## Follow-up / review
Document haemoglobin trend, haemodynamic status, transfusion/interventions, renal/hepatic function, concomitant antiplatelets/NSAIDs, dose, INR or relevant anticoagulation parameters, source of bleeding and outcome.

## Teaching point
A serious event can be **expected**. The seriousness decision is driven by hospitalisation; expectedness is driven by comparison with the RSI.
