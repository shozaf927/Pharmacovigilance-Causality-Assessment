# Case 02 — Severe Intensity, Non-serious, Unexpected Headache

## Case narrative
A 42-year-old adult taking fictional medicine **BX-204** reports a severe headache beginning 6 hours after the first dose. Pain prevents usual activities for most of the day. The patient is assessed in an outpatient clinic; neurological examination is normal, no emergency intervention is needed, and the patient is not admitted. Symptoms resolve the next day after symptomatic treatment. No persistent disability or other medically important feature is documented.

## Hypothetical RSI excerpt
“Dizziness and mild headache may occur. Severe headache and disabling headache are not described.”

This excerpt is fictional.

## Classification
| Dimension | Result | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Severity | Severe | Marked intensity and substantial temporary interference with usual activity |
| Seriousness | Non-serious on supplied facts | No inpatient admission, life-threatening status, death, persistent/significant disability, congenital anomaly or IME documented |
| Expectedness | Unexpected | The supplied RSI describes mild headache only; the reported severe/disabling intensity is not consistent with it |
| Causality | Not determined here | Timing is compatible but does not establish causation |

## Clinical impact
Substantial short-term functional impact, but no regulatory seriousness criterion is met in the information supplied. If additional clinical findings indicate a medically important event, reassess seriousness.

## Follow-up questions
- Was there sudden-onset “thunderclap” pain, focal deficit, seizure, fever or altered consciousness?
- Was emergency treatment or inpatient admission required?
- Did the headache recur after subsequent doses?
- Were other medicines, migraine history or secondary causes present?

## Teaching point
This is the key counterexample: **severe does not automatically mean serious**. Expectedness is separately assessed against the hypothetical RSI.
