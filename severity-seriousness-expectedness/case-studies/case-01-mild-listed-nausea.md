# Case 01 — Mild, Non-serious, Expected Nausea

## Case narrative
A 34-year-old adult begins fictional oral medicine **AX-101** for a chronic condition. On day 2, the patient reports mild nausea lasting approximately 30 minutes after each dose. The patient continues normal work and meals, requires no treatment, and has no vomiting, dehydration or clinical assessment. Symptoms resolve while treatment continues.

## Hypothetical RSI excerpt
“Common adverse reactions include nausea, usually mild and transient.”

This excerpt is fictional and is supplied solely to make the exercise assessable.

## Classification
| Dimension | Result | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Severity | Mild | Brief, tolerated, no meaningful functional limitation or intervention |
| Seriousness | Non-serious | No death, life-threatening status, inpatient admission, disability, congenital anomaly or IME identified |
| Expectedness | Expected | Nausea, including mild/transient nature, is described in the supplied RSI |
| Causality | Not determined here | Temporal association alone is insufficient for a definitive causal conclusion |

## Clinical impact
Low immediate clinical impact in this vignette. Monitor persistence, worsening, hydration and adherence. Reassess if symptoms progress or new findings emerge.

## Documentation points
Record onset relative to dosing, frequency, duration, effect on intake/activity, treatment, outcome and exact RSI version.

## Teaching point
An expected, non-serious event may still be reportable/collectable under the applicable pharmacovigilance process. Expectedness is not a reporting exemption.
