# Consolidated Case Classification Matrix

All cases are fictional. RSI excerpts are hypothetical. Causality is deliberately not adjudicated by this module.

| Case | Severity | Seriousness | Expectedness | Primary rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 — AX-101 nausea | Mild | Non-serious | Expected | Transient nausea listed; no seriousness criterion |
| 02 — BX-204 headache | Severe | Non-serious* | Unexpected | Disabling intensity not described; no seriousness criterion in supplied facts |
| 03 — CX-330 GI haemorrhage | Severe | Serious | Expected | Inpatient admission; hospitalised GI bleeding described in RSI |
| 04 — DX-415 angioedema | Severe | Serious | Unexpected | Life-threatening airway compromise and admission; manifestation absent from RSI |
| 05 — EX-520 arrhythmia | Mild subjective symptoms / significant objective finding | Serious | Unexpected | Inpatient admission for documented arrhythmia; RSI only describes palpitations |

*Case 02 is non-serious on the facts supplied. Reclassify if follow-up reveals a medically important event, life-threatening features, inpatient admission or another criterion.

## Key learning observations
1. Severity and seriousness are independent: Case 02 is severe but non-serious; Case 05 has mild subjective symptoms but is serious.
2. Seriousness and expectedness are independent: Case 03 is serious and expected; Case 04 is serious and unexpected.
3. Expectedness is reference-dependent: each teaching case supplies its own fictional RSI excerpt.
4. A case classification does not prove causality.
5. Reporting decisions require the applicable jurisdiction, setting, responsible party and current rules.

## Portfolio-quality note
These examples are intentionally constructed to demonstrate classification logic. They are not epidemiological evidence, actual product-label interpretations or patient-specific recommendations.
