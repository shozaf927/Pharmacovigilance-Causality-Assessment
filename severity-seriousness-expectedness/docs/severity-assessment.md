# Severity / Intensity Assessment

## Purpose
Severity describes how intense an event is. It should be graded from the clinical description, functional impact, objective findings and intervention required—not inferred solely from seriousness.

## Practical descriptive framework

| Grade | Working description | Example indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Mild | Noticeable but transient/easily tolerated | No or minimal interference with daily activity; no or minimal treatment |
| Moderate | Meaningful discomfort or functional interference | Limits some usual activity; may require treatment or clinical review |
| Severe | Marked intensity or major functional limitation | Prevents usual activity, substantial distress, or requires substantial symptomatic intervention |

This framework is a teaching aid, not a validated universal scale. Where a protocol, therapeutic area or organisation requires CTCAE, WHO toxicity grading, or another standard, use that exact version and its event-specific criteria. Do not invent a grade from a generic table.

## Factors to capture
- Symptom intensity and duration.
- Frequency, recurrence and progression.
- Objective measurements and laboratory values.
- Effect on work, mobility, sleep, eating and self-care.
- Treatment required and response.
- Whether dose interruption, reduction or discontinuation occurred.
- Clinical complications and recovery.

## Examples that demonstrate independence
- Severe headache, disabling for several hours but no hospitalisation, life-threatening status, disability or IME: may be **severe but non-serious**.
- Mildly symptomatic arrhythmia that causes inpatient admission for clinically necessary monitoring: may be **mild in intensity but serious** because hospitalisation criterion is met.
- A mild rash with no serious criterion: usually **mild and non-serious**, but expectedness still requires RSI comparison.

## CTCAE caution
CTCAE is a toxicity grading system used in defined clinical contexts. CTCAE grades should not be substituted for regulatory seriousness. A CTCAE Grade 3 event is not automatically a serious adverse event unless a seriousness criterion is met; conversely, a lower-grade event can still be serious if it meets one of those criteria.

## Documentation
Record the grading system, version, event term, grade, evidence and reviewer. If only a narrative description is available, use a descriptive grade and clearly state the limitation.
