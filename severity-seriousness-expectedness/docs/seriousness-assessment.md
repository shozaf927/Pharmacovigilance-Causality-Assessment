# Seriousness Assessment

## Regulatory criteria
Apply the seriousness criteria to the event's actual clinical course, not to the adjective used by the reporter.

### Criterion-by-criterion review
1. **Death:** Did the event result in death? Record date and cause as reported; do not assume causality.
2. **Life-threatening:** Was the patient at immediate risk of death when the event occurred? “Could have become fatal if worse” is insufficient by itself.
3. **Inpatient hospitalisation:** Was there formal admission to an inpatient facility, or prolongation of an existing admission, because of the event? Distinguish admission from an emergency-department visit, outpatient observation, or precautionary evaluation according to local definitions and the actual care setting.
4. **Persistent/significant disability or incapacity:** Did the event cause a substantial and lasting disruption of the person's ability to conduct normal life functions? Document duration, functional effect and clinical evidence.
5. **Congenital anomaly/birth defect:** Was a congenital anomaly or birth defect reported following exposure? Apply pregnancy-exposure follow-up requirements even where the outcome is not yet known.
6. **Other medically important event (IME):** Could the event jeopardise the patient or require intervention to prevent one of the outcomes above? Use documented medical judgement. Examples in ICH E2A include intensive treatment for allergic bronchospasm, blood dyscrasias or convulsions even without hospitalisation.

## Decision rule
- If **one or more** criteria are met: classify the event as **serious**.
- If none are met after adequate review: classify as **non-serious**.
- If key information is missing or contradictory: do not silently assume either category. Record “seriousness pending/unknown” according to the applicable system, seek follow-up, and escalate where the potential seriousness warrants it.

## Documentation checklist
- Criterion(s) met and supporting facts.
- Admission status, dates, and whether admission was inpatient.
- Whether the event caused prolongation of hospitalisation.
- Clinical evidence for life-threatening status or IME judgement.
- Disability duration and functional impact.
- Pregnancy and outcome details where relevant.
- Source of information, reviewer, date/time and follow-up needed.

## Seriousness is not an outcome score
A patient may recover fully from a serious event after hospitalisation. A patient may experience intense pain without meeting any seriousness criterion. Outcome, intensity and regulatory seriousness must each be documented separately.

## Important caution
A serious event is not automatically an ADR. Seriousness can be assigned to an AE before causality is established. Reportability and timelines are determined by the applicable regulation, study protocol and organisational SOP.
