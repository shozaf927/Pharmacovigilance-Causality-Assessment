# India and Global Reporting Context

## India — PvPI / IPC and MAH guidance
The Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission's Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI) encourages reporting of suspected ADRs irrespective of whether they are known or unknown, serious or non-serious, frequent or rare. Therefore, “expected” or “non-serious” must never be interpreted as “do not report”.

The IPC **Guidance Document for Marketing Authorisation Holders (MAHs) of Pharmaceutical Products** (2024 edition available from IPC) specifies reporting timelines for MAHs:
- Serious adverse events: within **15 calendar days** of receipt of information by the MAH.
- Non-serious adverse events: within **90 calendar days** of receipt of information by the MAH.

The guidance identifies reporting to the National Regulatory Authority (CDSCO) and NCC-PvPI/IPC. Verify the current official document, scope, product category, recipient details and subsequent amendments before operational use. These are MAH-specific requirements in that guidance; do not present them as universal timelines for every reporter or every Indian clinical-trial situation.

## Clinical trials and other pathways
Clinical-trial safety reporting may have separate statutory requirements, sponsor obligations, ethics committee reporting and protocol-specific procedures. ICH E2A's expedited reporting framework for clinical development includes:
- Fatal or life-threatening unexpected suspected adverse reactions: as soon as possible and no later than 7 calendar days after sponsor awareness, followed by a more complete report within 8 additional calendar days.
- Other serious unexpected suspected adverse reactions: no later than 15 calendar days.

These ICH E2A timelines must be interpreted within applicable regional law and the trial setting. Do not automatically apply them to routine post-marketing cases.

## Post-authorisation global framework
ICH E2D(R1) is the revised post-approval safety-data guideline, adopted at ICH Step 4 on 15 September 2025. The EU legal effective date is 18 March 2026. It updates definitions and standards for management/reporting of post-authorisation ICSRs and discusses evolving sources such as patient support programmes, market research, digital platforms and organised data collection systems. Regional implementation can differ; check the applicable authority's implementation strategy.

## Practical rule
Before calculating a deadline, answer:
1. Which country/region's law applies?
2. Is this a clinical trial or post-authorisation case?
3. Who has the reporting obligation (sponsor, MAH, investigator, healthcare professional)?
4. What product category and source are involved?
5. What is the relevant awareness/receipt date and minimum information threshold?
6. Which current regulation, guideline and SOP set the deadline and recipient?
