# Expectedness / Listedness Assessment

## 1. Select the correct reference safety information
Before deciding expectedness, identify:
- Product and formulation.
- Country/region and regulatory context.
- Clinical-development or post-authorisation setting.
- Correct reference document (e.g., current approved local product information/SmPC or Investigator's Brochure).
- Version/effective date applicable to the case and relevant reporting period.
- Internal SOP rules for reference-document control.

Do not compare a case against a random online label or an outdated document. For this teaching project, each fictional case supplies a short, explicitly hypothetical RSI excerpt.

## 2. Compare the reaction with the RSI
Assess:
- **Nature:** Is the reaction itself described?
- **Specificity:** Is the reported diagnosis or manifestation more specific than the listed term?
- **Severity:** Is the observed intensity/severity consistent with the information described?
- **Clinical context:** Does the RSI qualify the event by dose, route, population, duration or other conditions?

An event may be unexpected if its nature or severity is not consistent with the applicable information. A generic term such as “rash” may not adequately describe a more specific severe cutaneous reaction. Clinical and regulatory judgement is required.

## 3. Expectedness outcome
- **Expected / listed:** Reaction is consistent with the applicable RSI, including the relevant nature/specificity/severity considerations.
- **Unexpected / unlisted:** Reaction is not consistent with the applicable RSI.
- **Pending / unable to assess:** Correct RSI is unavailable, version is uncertain, or case details are insufficient to compare reliably. Seek the source document or follow-up; do not force a binary answer.

## 4. Do not confuse expectedness with
- **Frequency:** Rare does not automatically mean unexpected if the reaction is listed.
- **Causality:** Expectedness does not prove the product caused the event.
- **Severity:** A severe reaction may be expected if that severity is described; a mild reaction may be unexpected if its nature is unlisted.
- **Seriousness:** Seriousness is outcome/action based; expectedness is RSI based.

## 5. Special reporting context
ICH E2A addresses unexpectedness in clinical development using the applicable product information, commonly the Investigator's Brochure for an investigational product. Post-authorisation expectedness and reporting are governed by the applicable regional framework and product information. ICH E2D(R1), adopted in 2025 and effective in the EU from 18 March 2026, updates post-approval ICSR definitions and data-management standards. Follow local implementation and company procedures.

## 6. Audit trail
Record the exact reference document title, version/date, section/page, reaction term compared, rationale, decision, reviewer and assessment date. If a reference safety document changes, preserve the historical assessment and apply change-control rules rather than silently rewriting a prior case.
