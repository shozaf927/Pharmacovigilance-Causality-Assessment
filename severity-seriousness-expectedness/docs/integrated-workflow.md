# Integrated Case Classification Workflow

## Step 1 — Establish the case facts
Capture the minimum case narrative: identifiable patient, identifiable reporter, suspect product, event/reaction, onset, exposure dates, dose/route, indication, concomitant medicines, relevant history, investigations, treatment, outcome and source. Protect privacy; use coded identifiers only in teaching examples.

## Step 2 — Describe the event
Use a clinically meaningful diagnosis or symptom description. Preserve the reporter's verbatim term where required and avoid upgrading symptoms to diagnoses without evidence. Code using the organisation's MedDRA version and coding conventions when operating in a real PV system.

## Step 3 — Grade severity
Apply the specified grading framework. If no formal scale applies, use the descriptive mild/moderate/severe framework and document evidence and limitations.

## Step 4 — Assess seriousness
Evaluate every applicable seriousness criterion individually. Record the criterion met and evidence. If information is incomplete, initiate follow-up and use the system's pending/unknown pathway.

## Step 5 — Assess expectedness
Identify the correct, current RSI. Compare nature, specificity and severity. Record exact document/version/section and the rationale. If the RSI or details are missing, mark pending rather than guessing.

## Step 6 — Assess causality separately
Use the framework required by the study, organisation or authority (e.g., WHO–UMC). Do not infer causality from seriousness or expectedness. A serious unexpected event can still have an unrelated or unassessable relationship to the suspect product.

## Step 7 — Determine reporting pathway and clock
Identify jurisdiction, pre- or post-authorisation setting, product type, report source, sponsor/MAH responsibilities, minimum criteria and applicable local timelines. Do not apply one region's timeline globally. For India, consult current CDSCO/PvPI requirements and the relevant MAH guidance; for clinical trials, consult applicable New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, ethics committee and sponsor requirements.

## Step 8 — Quality review
Check internal consistency:
- Does the seriousness rationale name an actual criterion?
- Does the severity grade have clinical support?
- Is expectedness tied to the correct RSI version?
- Is causality kept separate?
- Are event dates, outcome and follow-up documented?
- Is the reporting deadline calculated from the correct awareness/receipt date under the applicable rule?

## Step 9 — Preserve traceability
Maintain source documents, assessment rationale, reviewer identity, timestamps, follow-up attempts, medical review and any later reassessment. Changes should be auditable.
