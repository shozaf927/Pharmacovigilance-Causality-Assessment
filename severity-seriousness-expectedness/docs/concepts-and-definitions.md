# Core Concepts and Definitions

## 1. Adverse event versus adverse drug reaction
An **adverse event (AE)** is an untoward medical occurrence following exposure to a medicinal product; a causal relationship is not necessarily established. An **adverse drug reaction (ADR)** implies at least a reasonable possibility of a causal relationship under the applicable framework. In spontaneous reporting, suspected reactions should be reported without waiting for proof of causality.

## 2. Severity
Severity describes the **intensity** of the clinical manifestation. Common descriptive grades are:
- **Mild:** transient or easily tolerated; minimal or no intervention.
- **Moderate:** sufficiently troublesome to interfere with usual activity and/or require medical intervention.
- **Severe:** marked intensity, substantial functional limitation or clinically significant distress.

These are descriptive examples, not a universal grading scale. Use a protocol-defined scale when one applies (for example, CTCAE in an oncology clinical trial) and document the version and grade. Do not automatically translate CTCAE grades into regulatory seriousness.

## 3. Seriousness
Seriousness is a regulatory classification based primarily on outcome or medically important event criteria. Under ICH E2A, an event is serious if it:
1. Results in death.
2. Is life-threatening at the time of the event.
3. Requires inpatient hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation.
4. Results in persistent or significant disability/incapacity.
5. Is a congenital anomaly/birth defect.
6. Is another medically important event that may jeopardise the patient or require intervention to prevent one of the listed outcomes.

The medically important event criterion requires medical judgement; it is not a catch-all for every event requiring treatment.

## 4. Expectedness
Expectedness asks whether the **nature, specificity and/or severity** of the reaction are consistent with the applicable reference safety information. The controlling document depends on context:
- Clinical development: commonly the Investigator's Brochure (IB) or other protocol-defined reference.
- Post-authorisation: the applicable approved product information / Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) or local prescribing information, as specified by the relevant regulatory framework and company procedure.

A reaction can be unexpected because its nature is absent, its clinical specificity is not adequately described, or its severity is greater than described. A broad term in a label does not necessarily make every more specific or more severe manifestation expected.

## 5. Causality is a separate question
A case can be serious and expected even when causality remains uncertain. Conversely, a non-serious unexpected event may be suspected to be drug-related. Record causality separately using an appropriate method (e.g., WHO–UMC or another required framework). Do not use Naranjo or WHO–UMC scores to determine seriousness or expectedness.

## 6. Four-way interpretation matrix

| Seriousness | Expectedness | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Serious | Expected | Meets serious criteria; reaction is consistent with RSI |
| Serious | Unexpected | Meets serious criteria; reaction is not consistent with RSI |
| Non-serious | Expected | Does not meet serious criteria; reaction is consistent with RSI |
| Non-serious | Unexpected | Does not meet serious criteria; reaction is not consistent with RSI |

Severity is recorded as a separate third axis, not folded into this matrix.

## 7. Common classification errors
- Calling an event serious solely because the patient describes it as “severe”.
- Calling an event non-serious because the patient recovered.
- Assuming every hospital or emergency-department visit is inpatient hospitalisation.
- Treating a potential risk of death as “life-threatening” when the patient was not actually at risk of death at the time.
- Declaring an event expected without checking the correct version and jurisdiction of the RSI.
- Treating an event as unexpected merely because it is not common; frequency and expectedness are different concepts.
- Assuming expected events are exempt from routine collection or reporting.
