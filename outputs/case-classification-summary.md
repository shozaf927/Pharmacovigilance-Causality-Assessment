# Case Classification Summary

The following are provisional teaching classifications based only on the fictional vignettes in this repository.

| Case | Naranjo score | Naranjo category | WHO–UMC category | Main limitation |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin-associated rash | 2 | Possible | Possible | Exact latency, diagnosis, alternative causes and dechallenge absent |
| Phenytoin-associated neurological toxicity | 7 | Probable | Probable / Likely | Numeric drug level, sampling time and full differential absent |
| Nifedipine-associated hypotension | 2 | Possible | Possible | No exact BP values, timeline or medication review |
| Paracetamol-associated liver injury | 3 | Possible | Possible | Dose, exposure timeline and liver injury differential incomplete |

## Interpretation
- Agreement between methods in these examples is incidental; the methods use different structures and should be independently applied.
- The phenytoin score assumes the reported toxic concentration is valid and that no alternative cause is identified in the vignette. If those assumptions change, the score may change.
- These are educational examples, not validated clinical case assessments.
- A real case may remain conditional/unclassified or unassessable if information is insufficient.

## Portfolio quality principle
A defensible assessment shows the evidence, assumptions, missing information and reasoning—not merely a category label.
